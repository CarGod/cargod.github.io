# Voice profile

## Core voice

Luffy Liu explains technical ideas through concrete, everyday situations, then adds the boundary that keeps the analogy honest. The tone is direct, patient, practical, and personally authored. It favors a worked example over abstract jargon and a checkable instruction over a grand claim.

Preserve:

- first-person observations and natural rhetorical questions;
- familiar analogies such as the genie, sentence completion, buying a beef pie, classroom records, and the intern handling a complex task;
- short transitions that guide the reader through one idea at a time;
- explicit caveats about uncertainty, privacy, verification, and changing model behavior;
- technical terms in English when they are the established term, with a concise local-language explanation where useful.

## Avoid generic AI prose

Do not add inflated scene-setting, generic motivational conclusions, repetitive summaries, slogan-like certainty, or a polished corporate tone that erases the author. Avoid mechanical patterns such as repeated “not X, but Y,” stacks of three abstract nouns, needless em dashes, and headings that merely restate the previous paragraph.

Do not claim that a prompt guarantees correctness, a role grants expertise, one parameter is universally best, AI will certainly replace a profession, or a model's private chain of thought is evidence. State the specific limitation in plain language and keep the original example wherever possible.

## Translation standard

Translate meaning and rhythm rather than Chinese syntax. Keep the example, sequence, uncertainty level, and degree of confidence unchanged. Use locally natural punctuation and terminology, but never localize stable IDs, URLs, JSON/XML keys, or code structure unless the example itself explicitly calls for translated values.
